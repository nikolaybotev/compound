# compound

A zero-dependency Node.js calculator for a fixed monthly mortgage payment and its amortization, and for the prepaid finance charge implied by a note rate and an APR. The skill at `.agents/skills/mortgage-loan-calculator/SKILL.md` asks for missing facts before it answers, for a fixed-rate loan and for a fixed-then-adjusting ARM. The design is `intent/mortgage-skill/`. The fee skill is `.agents/skills/mortgage-origination-fees/SKILL.md`. Its design is `intent/origination-fees/`.

## Conventions

- Docs follow the solo AI-native SDLC chain: `intent/<change>/intent.md` → `spec.md` → `plan.md`. Update `plan.md` in the same commit when implementation departs from it.
- The calculator has no npm dependencies. The page's dependencies live in `apps/web` and are installed with pnpm. Do not add a dependency to the calculator or to the `node --test` job.
- The human summary formats money in US locale with two decimal places, from integer cents. `--json` reports money as integer cents. The `--schedule` CSV money columns stay two decimal places with no grouping separators. In ARM mode the CSV adds `rate`, `payment`, and `index`: `payment` is money like the others, while `rate` and `index` print from thousandths with trailing zeros removed (`5.875`, `6.92`, `10.875`), never as two decimals, and `index` is empty when none was given.
- The note rate is percent per year (`6.99` means 6.99%), and the monthly rate is that percent divided by 12.
- There is no default loan. A missing or invalid `--amount`, `--rate`, or term flag exits non-zero and explains the reason on stderr. Exactly one of `--years` or `--months` is required; both or neither fails. No arguments names `--amount`, `--rate`, `--years`, and `--months`. `--json` always includes `months`; `years` appears only when `--years` was passed.

## Architecture

- `amortize.js` is the pure walk. It exports `buildReport(principal, ratePercent, monthCount, extrasByMonth, yearsForJson, arm)`, `dollarsToCents`, `formatGroupedCents`, and `formatPlainCents`. The sixth argument `arm` is optional: when it is `undefined` the function is the fixed-rate walk and the report has no `arm` key and no added row fields. When it is the ARM terms object (`fixedMonths`, `adjustMonths`, `marginThousandths`, `initialCapThousandths`, `periodicCapThousandths`, `lifetimeCapThousandths`, `floorThousandths`, `initialFloorThousandths`, `roundEighth`, `indexByMonth` as a `Map` of payment number to index thousandths), the report gains `arm` (`adjustments`, `max_rate_*`, `max_payment_*`, `ceiling_percent`) and each schedule row gains `rate_percent`, `payment_cents`, and `index_percent`. It has no Node APIs. `buildReport` always includes `schedule`. `years` is set on the report only when `yearsForJson` is passed. The page calls this module; it does not keep a second formula.
- `compound_interest_monthly.js` is the calculator CLI. It requires `amortize.js`, parses arguments (including the ARM flags and the `--index` CSV, which it turns into integer thousandths), reads `--extra`, and prints. A library `Error` from an ARM term becomes the non-zero exit with the library's message on stderr. It walks a 30/360 schedule, applies extra principal after that month's interest, and stops charging at payoff. The reported schedule still runs through the no-extra payoff; later rows are zeros. `--json` omits `schedule` unless `--schedule` is set.
- `fixtures/first-year-100.csv` is the $100-for-the-first-year example.
- `compound_interest_monthly.test.js` is the `node --test` suite.
- `.agents/skills/mortgage-loan-calculator/SKILL.md` is the front end. It asks for a missing principal, note rate, term, or extra plan, writes a `month,extra` CSV, and answers from `--json`. A month or savings-so-far question adds `--schedule` and reads `schedule`. For an ARM it also asks for the fixed period, adjustment interval, margin, three caps, and lifetime floor (the first-adjustment floor is optional), runs the worst case when no index is given, writes a `month,index` CSV at adjustment months only for an index path, and reads `arm.max_rate_percent`, `arm.max_payment_cents`, `arm.max_payment_month`, and `arm.adjustments` from `--json`. It does not amortize the loan itself.
- `intent/mortgage-skill/` is the spec and plan for that skill.
- `intent/arm-loan/` is the spec and plan for the fixed-then-adjusting ARM in the library, the CLI, the skill, and the page. Where `intent/mortgage-skill/` and `intent/loan-recast/` refuse an adjustable-rate loan, `intent/arm-loan/` wins for the ARM it describes. A recast of an ARM through the skill is still declined: the two-run procedure is for a fixed rate.
- `origination_fees.js` is the prepaid-finance-charge CLI. It uses `dollarsToCents` from `amortize.js` and does not walk a schedule. The payment is the note-rate payment. The amount financed is the present value of that payment at the APR. The charge is the note amount minus the amount financed, rounded once at output. `intent/origination-fees/` is the spec and plan. `.agents/skills/mortgage-origination-fees/SKILL.md` is the front end.
- `apps/web` is the amortization page. It imports `buildReport` and `formatGroupedCents` from `amortize.js`. A term in years is `monthCount = years * 12`, passed with the year count as `yearsForJson`. The extra column is the requested dollars; Apply replaces that map. Saved by extra is the marginal lifetime interest that month's requested extra saves given the rest of the committed map: `loanReport` without that month minus `loanReport` on the full map, using each run's `interest_cents`. It is not the row's cumulative `interest_saved_cents`. The column follows the committed map only; an uncommitted extra keystroke does not change it. The loan, the map, the prefill, the picture, and which years are open are stored under `compound-amortization-v1` on production and prototype. Feature preview builds use `compound-amortization-feat-<pull-request-number>-v1` via `VITE_STORAGE_KEY` so previews do not read or write production storage and open features do not share one loan. A fresh visit is the Conventional loan: purchase price $600,000, 5% down, 30 years, note rate 7.375%. The heading is `{thousands}K | {down}% down | {rate}% fixed = ${whole dollars} / month`. On that visit the heading complete monthly payment is $4,853 and the summary principal and interest is $3,936.85. In fixed mode the Monthly payment block has no note; the ARM block keeps `Initial payment. The extra payment is on top of this amount, and the payment resets at each adjustment.` When the extra map has any amount the summary shows `Extra principal paid` (the report's `extra_applied_cents`, the applied total, not the requested one) immediately before Interest saved. Payoff date is `{long date} ({years and months})` from `payoff_month` (`October 2056 (30 years)`), and Months saved is the bare count under twelve and years and months from twelve (`2 years 4 months`), both from `yearsAndMonths` in `loan.ts`. The start month is a page-drawn picker, not an `<input type="month">`: `button#start` shows the long date (`October 2026`) with `data-value="YYYY-MM"`, `aria-haspopup="dialog"`, and `aria-expanded`; it opens a `Choose start month` dialog with `Previous year` / `Next year` (years 1000-9999) and twelve month buttons named `{Month} {year}` (`aria-pressed` on the current one). Choosing a month commits through `update("start", …)`, closes, and refocuses the trigger; Escape and a pointer down outside the card, trigger, and its label close it; the label click toggles it. The chart legend is a tooltip: `position: fixed`, `pointer-events: none`, placed from an anchor in viewport coordinates (the pointer's `clientX`/`clientY` for a mouse move or a touch tap; for the keyboard, the indicated bar's center x and the chart's top edge) with its own measured size, a 16px gap, and an 8px window margin. It sits right of and below the anchor, flips to the left or above when it would leave the window, and is clamped otherwise, so it may leave the chart's box and may cover the anchor only when neither side fits. Because it takes no pointer events, the month under the pointer is always the indicated month. Make extra payments and Monthly payment and closing costs open from disclosure summaries. Apply sits in a `.apply-row` beside the sentence `Apply rewrites all extra-payment column values.`, which appears once; the old line above the form is gone. Inside `.field` a `select` has `appearance: none`, the inputs' font size, padding, and line height, and a data-URI chevron so its border and baseline line up with the amount fields in Safari; `#product` is excluded and keeps its native arrow. An extra cell is `data-edited="true"` (blue, `--edited` `#1d4ed8`, on the text and the bottom border) when its committed amount differs from the stored `applied` map for that month, which is the requested-extra map as the last Apply wrote it. Apply sets `applied` to the new map, so no cell is edited after it; a commit changes the attribute and a keystroke never does. `applied` is saved beside `extras` as `[[month, dollars], …]`; a missing or malformed key is read as equal to the loaded `extras`, and a term change drops months past the term from both. The year row's Saved by extra is the sum of that year's cells (`$0.00` with no extra), and the subtotals do not add up to Interest saved: on $100 in months 1–12 the year rows sum to `$8,071.88` while Interest saved is `$8,137.70`. Picture principal and interest is `monthly_payment_cents` from `buildReport` on the financed principal. The prototype stays at https://nikolaybotev.github.io/compound/prototype/. Vite's `base` defaults to `./`. Atkinson Hyperlegible and IBM Plex Mono live in `apps/web/fonts` with their SIL Open Font License files; the build fails, and names the file, if a shipped font's license is not that license.
- The page has an ARM mode (`apps/web/src/arm.ts`). The heading's static `fixed` is a `<select id="product">` with `fixed` and `ARM`; in ARM mode a structure label (`7/1` for 12 months, `7/6` for 6, otherwise `{years}/{months}mo`, so a 1-month interval is `7/1mo`) sits before the select. A fresh visit and every saved scenario without a well-formed `arm` object are `fixed`. Switching to ARM opens an "ARM terms" disclosure with the sheet's values (fixed period 7 years, adjusts every 12 months, margin 2.5, caps 5/2/5, lifetime floor 2.5, first adjustment floor 2.5, round to 1/8 off) and two computed lines, `Ceiling {rate + lifetime cap}%` and `First adjustment {long date} (payment {F + 1})`. The first switch opens it and stores `open: true`; after that the stored flag wins. ARM figures on the page are `buildReport` with the sixth `arm` argument: `loanReport` and `savedByExtraCents` pass it only while the product is ARM and pass `undefined` when it is `fixed`, even though the terms and the index stay in storage under `arm` (`enabled: false`). The `arm` key is written only once the owner has selected ARM or toggled the disclosure. The summary gains "Highest rate" and "Highest payment" (each `from {long date}`) in ARM mode only, and the Monthly payment note reads "Initial payment. The extra payment is on top of this amount, and the payment resets at each adjustment." The heading payment and the picture's principal and interest stay `monthly_payment_cents`, the initial payment. An invalid term entry names the field and keeps the last valid terms; a floor of 0 or above `rate + lifetime cap`, a fixed period not shorter than the term, and a rate edit that drops the ceiling under a floor are field errors, never a library throw. Switching to ARM with stored terms that no longer fit the loan is refused with that field error. In ARM mode the schedule has eleven columns: `#`, Date, Rate, Payment, Principal, Interest, Index, Extra payment, Saved by extra, Principal balance, Interest balance (the ten-column header is the CLI's CSV, not the table); fixed mode keeps today's eight. Rate is the row's `rate_percent` with `%` and Payment is its `payment_cents`; year rows leave Rate, Payment, and Index empty. The Index cell is an input only on adjustment rows (`aria-label` `Index for month N`), committed on blur or Enter with `^\d+(?:\.\d{1,3})?$`, not the dollar grammar: empty deletes the entry (the worst case returns), `0` is an index of zero, and invalid text reverts. A term, fixed-period, or interval change drops index entries that are no longer adjustment months. Apply replaces only the extra column. Saved by extra on an ARM runs `buildReport` with the same `arm` object on both runs. The table still ends at `payoff_month`.

## Things agents get wrong

- Do not put the $855,000 / 6.99% / 30-year gist loan back as constants that run when arguments are missing. That scenario is only `--amount 855000 --rate 6.99 --years 30`.
- Charging stops when the principal reaches zero. Do not keep accruing interest after payoff. The schedule still lists the later months through the no-extra payoff, with zeros, so the last `interest_saved` matches the summary.
- `Extra principal paid` is `extra_applied_cents`, the dollars that reduced principal. $100 on every month of the $570,000 / 7% / 30-year loan is `$33,200.00` (the loan is gone in month 332), not `$36,000.00`.
- Do not put `<input type="month">` back for the start month: Safari and Firefox render it as a text field. The picker is page-drawn so every browser sees one control.
- Do not make the Saved by extra year subtotals sum to Interest saved, and do not mark an extra cell edited on a keystroke: it is edited only when the committed map differs from `applied`.
- An extra payment does not reduce that month's interest. On $570,000 at 7%, month 1 interest is 332500 cents even when month 1 includes extra principal. Interest saved is cumulative against the no-extra schedule, so that same example saves 0 cents in month 1 and 813770 cents by month 360. Saved by extra, when the column exists, is a different number: the lifetime interest that one month's extra saves given the rest of the map. Do not fill it with `interest_saved_cents`.
- The skill asks when the principal, note rate, term, extra plan, or recast month is missing. It does not invent taxes, insurance, or an $855,000 loan. Summary questions use `--json` alone. A month or savings-so-far question is the only reason to add `--schedule` on a non-recast run. A recast uses `--json --schedule` on the first run only. An APR fee, points, or lender-credit question is `origination_fees.js`, not this script. On an ARM the skill never passes `--round-eighth` unless the user says the note rounds to an eighth, never passes `--initial-floor` unless the user gave one, writes the index (not the rate) in the CSV, and asks when "year k" or a calendar month is not an adjustment month instead of sliding it to the first one. It refuses interest-only, payment-option, and negative-amortization loans.
- Do not recompute an origination fee by rounding the payment first. $600,000 at 6.75% note and 7.21% APR for 30 years is a finance charge of 2725720 cents ($27,257.20) and a payment of 389159 cents ($3,891.59). $27,257.34 and $3,891.53 are the early-rounded figures and are wrong here. The fee skill asks for a missing note rate, APR, or loan size. It passes `--years 30` only when the user gave no term, and it says so. A negative `finance_charge_cents` is a lender credit. Monthly mortgage insurance and odd-days interest are not split out of that number.
- The skill runs `scripts/compound_interest_monthly.js`. That file is a symlink to the calculator at the repo root. The spec's `../../compound_interest_monthly.js` is one directory short. `plan.md` records that.
- Do not round currency with `Math.round(dollars * 100) / 100`. `Math.round(1.005 * 100)` is 100. Round reported amounts once, at output, half up to the cent.
- The schedule matches Bankrate's table, not a servicer ledger. Do not round the payment or each month's interest to the cent inside the walk. On $570,000 at 7% for 30 years the exact walk totals $795,200.72; a cent-rounded servicing walk is about $795,203.90. The README footnote states that on purpose.
- Commit `e67ec85` removed `Math.ceil` on the payment. Putting that ceiling back, or rounding the walk to cents, reopens a discrepancy the script was changed to close.
- The amortization page does not grow its own loan walk. It calls `buildReport`. That function takes a month count, not a year count: `buildReport(principal, ratePercent, monthCount, extrasByMonth, yearsForJson, arm)`, where `arm` is optional. A page term in years is `monthCount = years * 12`, and the year count is `yearsForJson`. The loan start month is not payment 1; payment 1 is the next calendar month. Apply replaces the extra column; it does not add to it.
- Do not add `apps/web/test/` or a `*.test.ts` file that `node --test` will discover. The page's unit tests live in `apps/web/unit/` and run with `pnpm --dir apps/web test`.
- The first ARM reset uses the initial cap, not the periodic cap. On 5/2/5 from 5.875% the rate is 10.875% at payment 85 (the 2/2/5 run is the one that steps 7.875%, 9.875%, 10.875%). A periodic-cap step at payment 85 understates the worst case.
- The ARM index CSV and the page's index cell take the index, not the rate. The walk adds the margin, optionally rounds to an eighth, and applies the caps and floors. `--round-eighth` is off unless the user asks. The floors must be greater than zero.
- An ARM reset re-amortizes the actual balance over the payments left, so extra principal lowers the next reset payment instead of shortening the term: $100 in months 1–12 on the First Entertainment 7/1 saves 357936 cents and 0 months, with a reset payment of 502039 cents.
- The fixed-rate JSON, CSV, and summary are unchanged by ARM support and a test pins them byte for byte. Do not add a key, a column, or a line to a fixed-rate run. `amortize.js` still has no Node APIs.
