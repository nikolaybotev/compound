## 1. The 3-Day Rule

**Citation:** [12 CFR § 1026.19(e)(1)(iii)(A)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-12/chapter-X/part-1026/section-1026.19#p-1026.19(e)(1)(iii)) · [CFPB](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1026/19/#e-1-iii) · [GovInfo](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/cfr/12/1026?sectionnum=19&year=mostrecent)

> The creditor shall deliver or place in the mail the disclosures required under paragraph (e)(1)(i) of this section not later than the third business day after the creditor receives the consumer's application, as defined in § 1026.2(a)(3).

The creditor must deliver those disclosures, or place them in the mail, no later than the third business day after it receives the consumer's application. "The disclosures required under paragraph (e)(1)(i)" are [the disclosures named "Loan Estimate"](#disclosures). "Application, as defined in § 1026.2(a)(3)" is [the definition of a complete application](#application).

<a id="disclosures"></a>

### The disclosures are the Loan Estimate

Paragraph (e)(1)(iii)(A) points at paragraph (e)(1)(i). That paragraph points at § 1026.37. Section 1026.37 requires the form to be titled "Loan Estimate."

**Citation:** [12 CFR § 1026.19(e)(1)(i)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-12/chapter-X/part-1026/section-1026.19#p-1026.19(e)(1)(i)) · [CFPB](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1026/19/#e-1-i) · [GovInfo](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/cfr/12/1026?sectionnum=19&year=mostrecent)

> In a closed-end consumer credit transaction secured by real property or a cooperative unit, other than a reverse mortgage subject to § 1026.33, the creditor shall provide the consumer with good faith estimates of the disclosures in § 1026.37.

**Citation:** [12 CFR § 1026.37](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-12/chapter-X/part-1026/section-1026.37) · [CFPB](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1026/37) · [GovInfo](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/cfr/12/1026?sectionnum=37&year=mostrecent)

> § 1026.37 Content of disclosures for certain mortgage transactions (Loan Estimate).
>
> For each transaction subject to § 1026.19(e), the creditor shall disclose the information in this section.

**Citation:** [12 CFR § 1026.37(a)(1)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-12/chapter-X/part-1026/section-1026.37#p-1026.37(a)(1)) · [CFPB](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1026/37/#a-1)

> The title of the form, "Loan Estimate," using that term.

**Citation:** [Official comment 19(e)(1)(iii)-5](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1026/Interp-19/#19-e-1-iii-Interp-5)

> Section 1026.19(e)(1)(iii) generally requires a creditor to deliver the Loan Estimate or place it in the mail not later than the third business day after the creditor receives the consumer's application and not later than the seventh business day before consummation.

The disclosures in [the 3-day rule](#three-day) are the good-faith estimates of the § 1026.37 disclosures, on the form titled "Loan Estimate." The official comment uses that name for the delivery duty in § 1026.19(e)(1)(iii). The same comment also states a second duty in that paragraph: delivery or mailing no later than the seventh business day before consummation.

## 2. The Definition of a Complete Application

**Citation:** [12 CFR § 1026.2(a)(3)(ii)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-12/chapter-X/part-1026/section-1026.2#p-1026.2(a)(3)) · [CFPB](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1026/2/#a-3-ii) · [GovInfo](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/cfr/12/1026?sectionnum=2&year=mostrecent)

> For transactions subject to § 1026.19(e), (f), or (g) of this part, an application consists of the submission of the consumer's name, the consumer's income, the consumer's social security number to obtain a credit report, the property address, an estimate of the value of the property, and the mortgage loan amount sought.

For a Loan Estimate transaction, an application consists of these six pieces:

1. The consumer's name.
2. The consumer's income.
3. The consumer's Social Security number to obtain a credit report.
4. The property address.
5. An estimate of the value of the property.
6. The mortgage loan amount sought.

**Citation:** [Official comment 2(a)(3)-1](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1026/Interp-2/#2-a-3-Interp-1)

> This definition does not prevent a creditor from collecting whatever additional information it deems necessary in connection with the request for the extension of credit. However, once a creditor has received these six pieces of information, it has an application for purposes of the requirements of Regulation Z.

Receiving the six pieces is enough to start [the 3-day rule](#three-day). The creditor may still collect other information. "An estimate of the value of the property" is [section 3](#estimate-of-value).

## 3. The "Estimate of Value" = Purchase Price Loophole Closure

**Citation:** Official Interpretations of Regulation Z, 12 CFR Part 1026, Supplement I, paragraph 2(a)(3)(ii)-1. That paragraph is not in [Supplement I](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-12/chapter-X/part-1026/appendix-Supplement%20I%20to%20Part%201026).

> 1. Estimated value. An estimate of the value of the property may be a valuation performed by a mortgage broker or an appraiser, or it may be the purchase price.

That sentence is not an official interpretation. The comments published under 2(a)(3) are "In general," "Social security number to obtain a credit report," and "Receipt of credit report fees." The regulation's words are "an estimate of the value of the property," in [the definition of a complete application](#application).

The published comment on the sale price and the purchase agreement is this:

**Citation:** [Official comment 19(e)(2)(iii)-1.i](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1026/19/#e-2-iii)

> A creditor may ask for the sale price and address of the property, but the creditor may not require the consumer to provide a purchase and sale agreement to support the information the consumer provides orally before the creditor provides the disclosures required by § 1026.19(e)(1)(i).

The creditor may ask for the sale price. It may not require a purchase and sale agreement before it provides [the disclosures named "Loan Estimate"](#disclosures). That prohibition is [section 4](#verification).

## 4. Prohibition on Requiring Verification Documents (e.g., Executed Purchase Contract)

**Citation:** [12 CFR § 1026.19(e)(2)(iii)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-12/chapter-X/part-1026/section-1026.19#p-1026.19(e)(2)(iii)) · [CFPB](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1026/19/#e-2-iii) · [GovInfo](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/cfr/12/1026?sectionnum=19&year=mostrecent)

> The creditor or other person shall not require a consumer to submit documents verifying information related to the consumer's application before providing the disclosures required by paragraph (e)(1)(i) of this section.

A creditor, or any other person, must not require verifying documents before providing the disclosures. The documents are those that verify information related to [the application](#application). The purchase and sale agreement is the example in [section 3](#estimate-of-value), not a phrase in this sentence. "The disclosures required by paragraph (e)(1)(i)" are [the disclosures named "Loan Estimate"](#disclosures).
