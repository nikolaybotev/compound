# Part F. The documents

<a id="part-f"></a>

The Loan Estimate's three-business-day delivery rule, the six pieces that make an application, and the ban on requiring verification documents first are the [TRID citation note](../TRID_LOAN_ESTIMATE_COMPLIANCE_CITATIONS.md). This page does not restate those sections. It says where ARM terms show up, and what only the note, the rider, or the security instrument contains.

<a id="program-disclosure"></a>

## F1. The ARM program disclosure and the CHARM booklet

For an ARM on a principal dwelling with a term over one year, the creditor must give the **CHARM booklet** (Consumer Handbook on Adjustable-Rate Mortgages) and a **loan program disclosure** for each ARM program the consumer is interested in. Both are due when the application form is given or before a non-refundable fee is paid, whichever comes first (or within three business days for phone or broker applications) (**Verified**, [12 CFR 1026.19(b)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-12/chapter-X/subchapter-B/part-1026/subpart-C/section-1026.19#p-1026.19(b))). The program disclosure covers, among other things:

- that the rate, payment, or term can change;
- the index and where it is published;
- how the rate and payment are set (index + margin);
- that the consumer should ask about the current margin and rate;
- any discount (teaser) on the start rate;
- how often the rate and payment change;
- the caps;
- a worst-case payment example;
- the rules on demand features, assumption, and conversion where they apply.

The program disclosure is that creditor's own document. It is often the first place the real caps, floor, lookback, and rounding can be read before the note.

<a id="le-cd"></a>

## F2. Where the ARM terms show up on the Loan Estimate and Closing Disclosure

- **Page 1, Loan Terms:** "Can this amount increase after closing?" for the interest rate and the principal and interest payment. Then Prepayment Penalty and Balloon Payment YES/NO (CHARM, "Use your Loan Estimate to understand your ARM").
- **Page 1, Projected Payments:** payment ranges by year band, showing the minimum and maximum after the initial period.
- **AIR table** (Adjustable Interest Rate): index + margin, initial rate, minimum/maximum rate, first-change timing, subsequent-change frequency, and first and subsequent change limits. It is on Loan Estimate page 2 under Closing Cost Details and on Closing Disclosure page 4 under Additional Information About This Loan (**Verified**, 12 CFR 1026.37(j); 12 CFR 1026.38(n)). The CHARM sample shows "1 Year CMT + 2.25%", "Beginning of 61st month", "2%/2%".
- **AP table** (Adjustable Payment): interest-only, optional payments, step payments, seasonal payments (12 CFR 1026.37(i); 12 CFR 1026.38(m)).
- **Loan Calculations** (Closing Disclosure page 5): Total of Payments, Finance Charge, Amount Financed, APR, and Total Interest Percentage. For an ARM these use the composite assumption described in [C5](qualifying.md#apr).

<a id="cd-vs-note"></a>

## F3. What's on the Closing Disclosure vs what's only in the note, the ARM rider, or the deed of trust

| Document | What it is | What only it contains (typical) |
|---|---|---|
| **Closing Disclosure** | The federal summary form. It must be received at least 3 business days before signing (12 CFR 1026.19(f)(1)(ii)). | Final costs, cash to close, APR, the AIR/AP tables, and YES/NO answers on page 4 (assumption, demand feature, late payment, negative amortization, partial payments, escrow). |
| **Promissory note** (ARM note) | The loan contract: the promise to repay. | The exact change dates, the index definition and fallback, the lookback, the rounding rule, the precise cap and floor wording, how prepayments are applied, the late-charge grace days and percent, default and acceleration notice, and any conversion option. |
| **Security instrument** (deed of trust in many states, or mortgage) | Pledges the home as collateral. | Escrow and insurance duties, occupancy, the **due-on-sale (transfer)** clause, acceleration and remedies including foreclosure (power of sale), and borrower reinstatement rights. |
| **ARM rider** | Attached to the security instrument. | Repeats the note's rate-change terms, and changes the transfer clause for an ARM (assumption conditions). |
| **Periodic statement** | The monthly servicer statement, after closing. | How each payment was applied (principal, interest, escrow), the next due date, and any suspense funds (12 CFR 1026.41). |

**Practical tip (Inference):** ask for **blank copies** of the note, ARM rider, and deed of trust forms well before closing. The Closing Disclosure summarizes. The note controls.

## F4. Acceleration, default, and due-on-sale

- **Acceleration** means the creditor demands the **entire** balance at once. The uniform note's default section: if a payment is missed, the note holder "may send me a written notice telling me that if I do not pay the overdue amount by a certain date, the Note Holder may require me to pay immediately the full amount of unpaid Principal… That date must be at least 30 days after the date on which the notice is mailed" (**Verified:** uniform ARM note, "Borrower's Failure to Pay as Required"). The security instrument sets out the formal notice, cure, and foreclosure steps, and state law adds its own.
- **Due-on-sale** is the other common acceleration trigger: transferring the property without consent, unless the loan is assumable on approval ([E4](prepaying.md#assumable)) or a federal exception applies.
- **Late charge vs default:** a payment is late after the grace period in the note (the uniform form leaves the number of days blank; 15 days is common on conventional loans, which is an **Inference**, not verified here) and gets a late charge. Default and acceleration are separate, more serious steps.
