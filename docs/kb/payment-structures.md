# Part B. Payment structures

<a id="part-b"></a>

The concepts skill's Types section already explains fully amortizing, interest-only, and option loans, with the page's general scenario (purchase price $600,000, 5% down, loan $570,000) and one CFPB sample. Those illustrations are not repeated here. This page adds the Regulation Z quotations, one separate illustration, and the balloon payment, which that skill does not explain.

<a id="amortizing"></a>

## B1. Fully amortizing

Each payment covers that month's interest plus enough principal to pay the loan off by the maturity date. On an ARM, the payment is recalculated at each change date over the remaining term. The concepts skill's procedure is that payment step. This page does not restate the formula.

Regulation Z calls a loan whose payments never increase the balance an "amortizing loan" (**Verified**, [12 CFR 1026.18(s)(7)(v)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-12/chapter-X/subchapter-B/part-1026/subpart-C/section-1026.18)).

<a id="interest-only"></a>

## B2. Interest-only

**Citation:** [12 CFR 1026.18(s)(7)(iv)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-12/chapter-X/subchapter-B/part-1026/subpart-C/section-1026.18)

> One or more of the periodic payments may be applied solely to accrued interest and not to loan principal.

That definition is **Verified**. The balance stays flat during the interest-only period. Afterwards the payment jumps, even if rates do not change, because the principal must then be repaid over fewer years (CHARM, "Interest-only ARMs"). The concepts skill's Types section is the page illustration and the CFPB sample of that jump's shape. It does not compute the later payment.

> **Illustration.** $400,000 at 6.25% interest-only = 400,000 × 0.0625 / 12 = **$2,083.33** a month, against $2,462.87 fully amortizing on the same illustration in [A7](arm-rate.md#arm-example). The difference is the principal that is not being repaid. This is not a reader's loan and not the page's $600,000 scenario.

<a id="negative-amortization"></a>

## B3. Negative amortization and payment-option ARMs

Negative amortization means "payment of periodic payments that will result in an increase in the principal balance" (**Verified**, [12 CFR 1026.18(s)(7)(v)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-12/chapter-X/subchapter-B/part-1026/subpart-C/section-1026.18)). A payment-option ARM lets the borrower choose a minimum payment that can be below the interest due. The unpaid interest is added to the balance (CHARM, "Payment option ARMs"). These loans largely disappeared after 2008. A loan with negative amortization cannot be a general qualified mortgage (see [C3](qualifying.md#qm)). The concepts skill explains the option and does not compute a minimum payment.

<a id="balloon"></a>

## B4. Balloon payment

Regulation Z defines a balloon payment as "a payment that is more than two times a regular periodic payment" (**Verified**, [12 CFR 1026.18(s)(5)(i)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-12/chapter-X/subchapter-B/part-1026/subpart-C/section-1026.18)). The Loan Estimate and Closing Disclosure page 1 answer "Balloon Payment: YES/NO". General qualified mortgages cannot have one (with narrow small-creditor exceptions).

**Where to check on a loan:** Loan Estimate page 1 "Loan Terms" (Prepayment Penalty, Balloon Payment), and the Loan Estimate page 2 / Closing Disclosure page 4 **AP table** (it answers whether there are interest-only payments, optional payments, or step payments) (**Verified**, [12 CFR 1026.37(i)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-12/chapter-X/subchapter-B/part-1026/subpart-C/section-1026.37) and [12 CFR 1026.38(m)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-12/chapter-X/subchapter-B/part-1026/subpart-C/section-1026.38)).

The three-business-day Loan Estimate delivery rule and what counts as an application are the [TRID citation note](../TRID_LOAN_ESTIMATE_COMPLIANCE_CITATIONS.md). This page does not restate them.
