## 1. Summary

`amortize.js` learns to walk a fixed-then-adjusting ARM: an initial rate for a fixed number of payments, then a rate reset every `A` payments, each reset bounded by an initial or periodic cap, a lifetime ceiling, and a floor, with the payment re-amortized at each reset over the payments left to maturity. With no index given, every reset takes the worst case the note allows. An index value supplied for an adjustment month replaces that assumption for that reset: the rate becomes index plus margin, passed through the same caps and floors. `compound_interest_monthly.js` exposes the terms as flags and the index path as a `month,index` CSV, and reports the reset list as `arm.adjustments` in `--json`; `--json` still omits the month-by-month `schedule` unless `--schedule` is set. The skill lifts its ARM refusal for this product and asks for the terms. The page gains an ARM mode: the terms, a worst-case summary, rate and payment columns, and an editable index cell on each adjustment row beside the extra-payment cell. The fixed-rate loan, its JSON, its CSV, and its page stay as they are.

This folder builds on [intent/mortgage-skill/](../mortgage-skill/spec.md), [intent/loan-recast/](../loan-recast/spec.md), [intent/amortization-app/](../amortization-app/spec.md), [intent/extra-savings-column/](../extra-savings-column/spec.md), and [intent/complete-loan-picture/](../complete-loan-picture/spec.md). Where those specs refuse an adjustable-rate loan, this spec wins for the ARM described in requirement 1. Everything else in them stands.

## 2. Goals and non-goals

**Goals**

- G1. Amortize a fixed-then-adjusting ARM from its terms: initial rate, fixed period, adjustment interval, margin, initial/periodic/lifetime caps, lifetime floor, and first-adjustment floor.
- G2. Default to the worst case the terms allow, with the exact cap sequence from the First Entertainment sheet and Ed's email: the initial cap at the first adjustment, the periodic cap after that, never above the lifetime ceiling.
- G3. Let a person replace the worst-case assumption at any adjustment month with an index value, in the CLI through a CSV and on the page in the schedule row next to the extra payment.
- G4. Report the reset schedule, the highest rate, and the highest payment so a summary question needs `--json` alone.
- G5. Keep extra principal working on an ARM, including interest saved and Saved by extra.
- G6. Lift the skill's ARM refusal for this product and have it ask for the terms it needs.
- G7. Add ARM mode to the page without a second formula and without changing the fixed-rate page.

**Non-goals**

- Interest-only, payment-option, negative-amortization, balloon, or graduated-payment ARMs. A payment cap (a limit on the payment change rather than the rate change). Any product without a fixed period followed by regular resets.
- Fetching index values from FRED, the New York Fed, or anywhere else. The calculator does no network access. Index values are typed by the person.
- Converting a calendar date, a lookback period, or an index name into an index value. Those tell a person which published figure applies; they do not change the arithmetic (D9).
- The upfront buy-down limit. It is a lender pricing rule on the starting rate, not a term of the walk (D9).
- APR, the prepaid finance charge, or points on an ARM. `origination_fees.js` and its skill are unchanged.
- A servicer recast of an ARM through the skill's two-run procedure (D13).
- Changing the fixed-rate `--json` object, the fixed-rate `--schedule` CSV, the fixed-rate summary text, or the fixed-rate page.
- Redesigning the Pages deploy. The queued UI follow-ups.

**Release phasing.** Every untagged requirement is this release. One item is tagged later and is not built now: **L1**, a page field that fills the same index into every adjustment month at once. The CLI covers that case today with a CSV that has one row per adjustment month, and the skill writes that CSV.

## 3. Principles

- P1. One walk. The ARM lives in `amortize.js` as a mode of `buildReport`. The CLI, the skill, and the page call it. The page does not amortize, and the skill does not type a payment formula.
- P2. Fixed stays fixed. With no ARM terms, `buildReport`, the JSON, the CSV, the summary, and the page produce the same bytes as before this change.
- P3. Worst case means the note's maximum at every reset, given everything the person has entered. An index at one adjustment changes that reset; later resets without an index resume the worst case from the new rate.
- P4. Rate arithmetic is exact. Percents are parsed to integer thousandths of a percent and caps, ceilings, floors, and index plus margin are added and compared as integers. The walk converts the chosen thousandths to a monthly rate once. Money is still rounded half up to the cent once, at output.
- P5. A reset re-amortizes the unpaid balance at the new rate over the payments left to maturity, which is what the uniform notes say (D5). The balance is the actual balance, so extra principal lowers the next reset payment.
- P6. The skill asks for a missing term and does not invent one. It does not look up an index.
- P7. Caps, floors, and the ceiling bound the index path; they do not reject it. A typed index that implies a rate above the ceiling produces the ceiling. The report shows both the index that was asked for and the rate that resulted.

## 4. Users and scenarios

- The owner runs the First Entertainment 7/1 ARM: $570,000, 5.875%, 30 years, fixed 7 years, annual resets, margin 2.50%, caps 5/2/5, floor 2.50%. The summary says the payment is $3,371.77 for 84 months, the rate becomes 10.875% at payment 85, and the payment becomes $5,037.71 from then on. Total interest is $1,103,636.33.
- The owner asks what the same loan costs if the 1-year CMT is 4.42% when the first reset is set. The skill writes `85,4.42`, and the reset rate is 6.92% with a payment of $3,695.72. The next reset, with no index given, goes to 8.92%, and the one after that to the 10.875% ceiling.
- The owner asks what happens if the index is 4.00% at every reset. The skill writes one row per adjustment month, and every reset is 6.50% with a payment of $3,563.80.
- On the page, the owner switches the heading product to ARM. The ARM terms open with the sheet's values, the heading reads `600K | 5% down | 5.875% 7/1 ARM = $4,288 / month` once the rate is 5.875, the summary adds the highest rate and payment, and the 2033 schedule rows show the rate jump. The owner types `4.42` in the Index cell of payment 85, and the rest of the path recomputes from 6.92%.
- The owner adds $100 extra in each of the first 12 payments. On this ARM that saves $3,579.36 of interest and 0 months: the lower balance at payment 85 lowers the reset payment to $5,020.39 instead of ending the loan sooner.

## 5. Functional requirements

1. **The product.** An ARM, for this repo, is a fully amortizing loan of `n` payments with: an initial rate `R0` (`--rate`); a fixed period of `F` payments at `R0`, `1 ≤ F < n`; resets at payments `F + 1`, `F + 1 + A`, `F + 1 + 2A`, … up to `n`, where `A ≥ 1` is the adjustment interval in months; a margin `M`; caps `C1` (initial), `Cp` (periodic), `CL` (lifetime); a lifetime floor `FL`; and a first-adjustment floor `F1`. Rates and caps are percents with at most three decimal places. The ceiling is `R0 + CL`. Payment `F + 1` is the first payment at a reset rate, and a reset rate stays in effect until the next reset. Month 1 is the first payment, as everywhere else in this repo.

2. **Rate at a reset.** Let `Rc` be the rate in effect before reset `j` (`j = 1` is the first reset). Let `cap_j` be `C1` when `j = 1` and `Cp` otherwise. Let `upper_j = min(Rc + cap_j, R0 + CL)`. Let `lower_1 = F1` and, for `j > 1`, `lower_j = max(Rc − Cp, FL)`. The target `T` is `upper_j` when no index is given for that month (the worst case), and `index + M` when one is, optionally rounded per requirement 4. The new rate is `max(min(T, upper_j), lower_j)`. All of this is integer arithmetic in thousandths of a percent.

3. **Worst case, stated as the sheet's rule.** With no index anywhere, reset 1 moves the rate up by the initial cap and every later reset moves it up by the periodic cap, each time stopping at the ceiling. On 5/2/5 from 5.875% the first reset reaches 10.875% and every later reset stays there. On 2/2/5 from 5.875% the resets are 7.875%, 9.875%, 10.875%, then 10.875%. The periodic cap does not govern the first reset (D4).

4. **Rounding to an eighth.** Off by default. With `--round-eighth` (CLI) or the page's "Round to nearest 1/8 point" box, `index + M` is rounded to the nearest multiple of 125 thousandths before the caps and floors apply. Caps and floors are not rounded, and the worst-case target is not rounded. With at most three decimals on the index and the margin, a sum is never exactly halfway between two eighths, so the tie rule never fires; the implementation still rounds half up. 4.42 + 2.50 = 6.92 rounds to 6.875. 4.437 + 2.50 = 6.937 rounds to 6.875 (D6).

5. **Payment.** Payment 1 through `F` is the existing level payment on the principal at `R0` over `n` payments. At reset month `m`, the payment for `m` onward is the level payment that amortizes the balance after payment `m − 1` at the new rate over `n − m + 1` payments. The balance is the walk's actual balance, including extra principal already applied. The payment is recomputed before that month's interest is charged, and interest for month `m` is that balance times the new monthly rate. Rates in the walk are `thousandths / 100000 / 12`.

6. **Walk, extras, and payoff.** Everything else in the walk stays: exact interest on the unpaid balance, extra principal applied after that month's interest, charging stops at payoff, later rows are zeros through the no-extra payoff. A reset falls at the start of its month, so a reset in the payoff month is applied and listed (F11's month 181); a reset after the payoff month does nothing and is not listed. The baseline for `interest_saved_cents` and `months_saved` is the same ARM, the same index map, and no extras; its payoff month is `n`. `interest_saved_cents` on a schedule row stays the cumulative difference against that baseline. `monthly_payment_cents` at the top level stays the initial payment, and top-level `rate_percent` stays the initial `--rate`; the rate charged in a given month is that row's `rate_percent`.

7. **Library.** `buildReport(principal, ratePercent, monthCount, extrasByMonth, yearsForJson, arm)` takes an optional sixth argument. When it is `undefined`, the function is unchanged and the report has no `arm` key. Otherwise `arm` is `{ fixedMonths, adjustMonths, marginThousandths, initialCapThousandths, periodicCapThousandths, lifetimeCapThousandths, floorThousandths, initialFloorThousandths, roundEighth, indexByMonth }` where `indexByMonth` is a `Map` from payment number to index thousandths. `ratePercent` stays a number; the library takes `R0` in thousandths as `Math.round(ratePercent * 1000)`, which is exact because the CLI and the page already limit the rate to three decimals. The library validates: `1 ≤ fixedMonths < monthCount`; `adjustMonths ≥ 1`; caps and margin `≥ 0`; floors `> 0`; each floor `≤ ceiling`; every `indexByMonth` key is a reset month from requirement 1 and `≤ monthCount`; each index `≥ 0`. A violation throws an `Error` whose message names the term, and the CLI turns that into a non-zero exit with that message on stderr. `amortize.d.ts` declares the argument, the `arm` report object, and the added row fields. `amortize.js` keeps having no Node APIs.

8. **Report additions in ARM mode.** The report gains `arm`:

   ```
   arm: {
     fixed_months, adjust_months,
     margin_percent, initial_cap_percent, periodic_cap_percent, lifetime_cap_percent,
     floor_percent, initial_floor_percent, ceiling_percent, round_eighth,
     max_rate_percent, max_rate_month, max_payment_cents, max_payment_month,
     adjustments: [{ month, index_percent, fully_indexed_percent, rate_percent, payment_cents }]
   }
   ```

   Percent fields are decimal numbers from thousandths (`6920` → `6.92`). `adjustments` lists every reset month from `F + 1` through the payoff month, in order; when extras pay the loan off early the list is shorter than the resets through `n`, and its length is what the summary's `Adjustments` line prints. `index_percent` and `fully_indexed_percent` are `null` for a worst-case reset; `fully_indexed_percent` is the sum after requirement 4's rounding when it is on. `rate_percent` is the rate chosen by requirement 2; `payment_cents` is that reset's payment. `max_rate_month` and `max_payment_month` are the first month at which the maximum rate and the maximum payment apply; `max_payment_month` is 1 when no reset payment exceeds the initial payment. Every schedule row gains `rate_percent` (the rate charged that month; after payoff, the last rate in effect), `payment_cents` (that month's scheduled payment; 0 after payoff), and `index_percent` (the index given for that month, or `null`). No field is added to a fixed-rate report.

9. **CLI flags.** ARM mode is on when any of these flags is present:

   ```bash
   node compound_interest_monthly.js \
     --amount 570000 --rate 5.875 --years 30 \
     (--fixed-years 7 | --fixed-months 84) --adjust-months 12 \
     --margin 2.5 --caps 5/2/5 --floor 2.5 \
     [--initial-floor 2.5] [--round-eighth] [--index index.csv] \
     [--extra extras.csv] [--json] [--schedule]
   ```

   `--fixed-years` and `--fixed-months` take the same `^[1-9]\d*$` text as `--years` and `--months`; exactly one is required in ARM mode, and both or neither exits non-zero with a message naming both flags. `--adjust-months` is `^[1-9]\d*$`. `--margin`, `--floor`, and `--initial-floor` are percents, `^\d+(?:\.\d{1,3})?$`; the margin may be `0`, the floors must be greater than zero. `--caps` is three such percents joined by `/`, each allowed to be `0`, in the sheet's order initial/periodic/lifetime. `--initial-floor` defaults to `--floor`. `--round-eighth` is a boolean. In ARM mode, `--adjust-months`, `--margin`, `--caps`, and `--floor` are required; a missing one exits non-zero and names every missing ARM flag in one message. `--index`, `--initial-floor`, or `--round-eighth` without the required ARM flags is that same error. A fixed-rate run is unchanged: no ARM flag, no ARM validation, same output. `--rate` stays the note rate; in ARM mode it is the initial rate. Duplicate flags still fail.

10. **Index CSV.** `--index FILE` has the header `month,index`. `month` is an integer payment number that must be a reset month within the term; any other month exits non-zero and says the month is not an adjustment month. `index` is `^\d+(?:\.\d{1,3})?$`, zero allowed. A duplicate month exits non-zero at CSV parse time, before the library runs; indexes do not sum, and a `Map` cannot carry two values for one month, so the library never sees a duplicate. The same BOM, blank-line, and column-count rules as `--extra` apply. An index for the payoff month is applied, because that reset happens at the start of the month (requirement 6): it changes that month's rate, payment, and interest. An index for a reset month after the payoff month is accepted, shown on that row's `index_percent`, and has no effect, since the balance is already zero.

11. **Summary and CSV in ARM mode.** The human summary keeps its seven lines and appends `Initial rate: 5.875%`, `Highest rate: 10.875% (from month 85)`, `Highest payment: 5,037.71 (from month 85)`, and `Adjustments: 23`, where the count is `arm.adjustments.length` (through the payoff month). Percents print from thousandths with trailing zeros removed (`5.875`, `6.92`, `10.875`), never as two decimals. The `--schedule` CSV header becomes the ten columns `month,interest,principal,remaining_principal,remaining_interest,extra,interest_saved,rate,payment,index`; `rate` and `index` print as those percents (`index` empty when none); `payment` and every other money column print as two decimals with no grouping separators, the existing CSV convention. The fixed-rate summary and CSV are byte-for-byte unchanged.

## 7. Design decisions

**D1 — A mode of the one walk, not a second module.** `buildReport` gains an optional `arm` argument. With it `undefined`, the code path is the current one and the output is unchanged (AC1). This keeps P1 and P2 and keeps the page and the skill on one function. A separate `amortizeArm` was rejected because the page and the fixed walk would then have two payoff and interest-saved definitions to keep aligned.

**D2 — The sheet's terms, as Ed stated them.** Checked 2026-10-10 against both PDFs. The parameter sheet and Ed's email (printout pages 23–24) agree: fixed 7 years, annual adjustments, 1-Year CMT, margin 2.50%, caps 5/2/5 glossed by Ed as "5% initial adjustment cap, 2% annual adjustment cap, 5% lifetime cap", initial floor 2.50%, lifetime floor 2.50%, lookback 45 days; the same terms for the 10/1 with a 10-year fixed period. The 5.50% figure is an upfront buy-down limit on the starting rate, not a floor in the walk; the earlier "5.5% floor" in the transcript was clarified to that. The 5.875% zero-point par rate and the $570,000 principal come from the transcript's reading of the rate sheet; they are the example, not defaults.

**D3 — Reset timing counts payments.** Resets are payments `F + 1`, `F + 1 + A`, …. The Fannie Mae notes (Forms 3501 and 3108, checked 2026-10-10) set the Change Date on the first day of a month and make the new payment due "beginning on the first monthly payment date after the Change Date", which is this count when payment 1 is the first payment after closing. A promissory note whose Change Date falls a month earlier or later than the fixed-year anniversary can be modeled with `--fixed-months`. Calendar dates stay outside the script, as in `intent/loan-recast` D4.

**D4 — The first reset uses the initial cap.** Ed's gloss names a 5% initial adjustment cap and a 2% annual adjustment cap, and the Fannie Mae note's Section 4(D) bounds the first Change Date absolutely ("will not be greater than ___% or less than ___%") and limits later changes by the periodic amount. The CFPB handbook defines the periodic cap as the limit "from one adjustment period to the next after the first adjustment". So the worst case on 5/2/5 is the ceiling at the first reset (F2), not a 2-point step (F3 is the 2/2/5 run). The intent's constraint says the rate "rises by the applicable cap at each adjustment" and that the exact rule is the sheet and Ed's email; requirements 2 and 3 are that rule. A builder should not implement a periodic-cap step at payment 85: that would understate the First Entertainment worst case by $26,515.99 of interest (F2 minus F3) and show 7.875% instead of 10.875% at payment 85.

**D5 — A reset re-amortizes the actual balance.** The notes say the new payment is the amount "sufficient to repay the unpaid principal that I am expected to owe at the Change Date in full on the Maturity Date at my new interest rate in substantially equal payments". The walk uses the balance after payment `m − 1`, which includes extra principal, over `n − m + 1` payments. The consequence is F10: $100 a month for a year saves $3,579.36 and 0 months on this ARM, where the same extras on a fixed 7% loan save $8,137.70 and end the loan 2 months early. That is the product, not a defect. A scheduled-balance reset (ignoring extras) was rejected because a servicer's ledger has one balance.

**D6 — Rounding to an eighth is optional and off.** The uniform notes round index plus margin "to the nearest one-eighth of one percentage point (0.125%)". Neither PDF says whether First Entertainment's note does. The worst case is unaffected either way, since the caps are whole points and the ceiling is `R0 + CL`. So rounding is a flag, off by default, applied only to an index target, and recorded in `arm.round_eighth`. With three-decimal inputs no sum is a tie, so "nearest" is unambiguous (F9). The skill does not pass it unless told.

**D7 — Caps bound both directions after the first reset; floors are absolute.** The notes: "never be increased or decreased on any single Change Date by more than" the periodic amount, and "never be greater than ___% or less than the Margin" (later forms) or a stated floor. The sheet gives two floors as rates, so `lower_1` is the initial floor exactly and `lower_j` for `j > 1` is `max(Rc − Cp, FL)`. F6 shows the step floor winning over the absolute floor. A note that limits the first decrease by the initial cap instead is modeled by setting the first-adjustment floor to `R0 − C1`. A negative index is rejected rather than deemed zero; the notes deem it zero, and a person modeling that case types `0`.

**D8 — The editable cell is the index, not the rate.** The transcript's whole arithmetic is index plus margin through caps and floors, and the intent asks to tweak "the rate adjustments". The index is the exogenous number: the same index path can be run against 7/1 and 10/1 terms, or against a different margin, without retyping. It also means nothing a person types is invalid: a high index produces the ceiling and the report shows both numbers (P7, F7). A typed note rate was rejected because a rate outside the caps would have to be refused or silently changed, and because it hides the carry-forward effect of a cap. When a person has a rate rather than an index, the skill writes `rate − margin` and says so.

**D9 — Which sheet rows are arithmetic.** Fixed period, adjustment frequency, margin, caps, and the two floors enter requirement 2. The index name and the lookback identify which published figure to read and on which date; the calculator does not read it, so they are not flags. The upfront buy-down limit constrains what starting rate the lender will originate; `--rate` is whatever the person was quoted, and the calculator does not check it. Adding display-only flags for those three was rejected because the repo's flags all do something, and the skill can repeat the terms from the conversation.

**D10 — Integer thousandths for every percent.** `6.99 + 2` in doubles is `8.990000000000002`. Parsing to thousandths and comparing integers makes caps, floors, and the ceiling exact and lets the CSV and the page print `6.92`, not `6.920000000000001`. The page already has `percentThousandths`. The monthly rate is `thousandths / 100000 / 12`, which for 5.875 is the same double as the fixed walk's `(5.875 / 100) / 12`, so the fixed period of an ARM matches the fixed loan to the cent (F1 against F2's initial payment).

**D11 — One index CSV, one meaning.** `month,index` with rows only at reset months. A row elsewhere is an error, not a silently ignored row, because the person most likely mis-counted the month. Duplicates are an error because two indexes for one date have no sum. This mirrors `--extra`'s strictness on months while differing where the meaning differs.

**D12 — The report carries the reset list.** `arm.adjustments`, `max_rate_*`, and `max_payment_*` let a summary question be answered from `--json` alone (P1 of the mortgage-skill spec). Per-row `rate_percent`, `payment_cents`, and `index_percent` let the page and a month question read the schedule without recomputing. `monthly_payment_cents` stays the initial payment so existing readers keep their meaning; the highest payment has its own field.

**D13 — No ARM recast through the skill.** The two-run recast procedure re-amortizes once at one rate. An ARM re-amortizes at every reset by requirement 5, so "what will my payment be after a curtailment" on an ARM is answered by the next reset's `payment_cents` with the extra on the schedule. A recast inside the fixed period is possible in principle but needs the second run to carry the ARM terms with a shifted reset schedule; it is out of this release. The skill declines in one sentence.

**D14 — Page defaults are the sheet; the product default is fixed.** A fresh visit is unchanged. The ARM terms section opens with Ed's values so the owner's loan is one product switch and one rate edit away (F14). The product select replaces the static word `fixed` so the fixed heading still reads the same way. Index name and lookback do not appear on the page (D9).

**D15 — Index cell beside the extra cell; Rate and Payment on the row.** The intent asks for the override "right next to where we enter extra payments" and for a table that shows the rate in effect and the payment each month. Index sits immediately before Extra payment; Rate and Payment sit after Date so the cause and both effects are visible on the row. Payment comes from the row's `payment_cents`, which the JSON and the CSV already carry; it adds no CSV column. Fixed mode keeps today's eight columns so existing checks and screenshots stay valid. A per-row rate input was rejected (D8).

**D16 — Later: fill every reset at once (L1).** A flat index path is the most common scenario and is 23 cells on a 7/1. The CLI and the skill cover it with a CSV. A page field for it is tagged L1 and is not part of this release's requirements or acceptance.

**D17 — Storage stays version 1.** `arm` is an additive key with a defined fallback, like `picture` in `intent/complete-loan-picture` requirement 6. Feature-preview keys are untouched.

**D18 — Floors must be positive.** `--rate` already must be greater than zero, and the payment formula divides by `(1 + r)^N − 1`. Requiring the floors to be positive keeps every reset rate positive without a zero-rate branch. Ed's floors are 2.50%, and the uniform notes' floor is the margin.
