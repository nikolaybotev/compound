## 1. Summary

The amortization page stores more than one loan scenario under the same localStorage key and switches the one being edited from a page-drawn list at the top. The list adds a scenario and removes one after confirmation. Export writes the whole set as one JSON file; import replaces the set when the file passes the same checks a load uses, and leaves the set alone when it does not. Each scenario has a prevailing monthly total: the principal-and-interest payment in effect for the most schedule months, the picture's monthly taxes and insurance, and a prevailing extra when one applied extra covers at least 80 percent of those months. The heading shows that total in whole dollars. A three-color bar beside the amount shows the shares. The picture and the schedule both show the prevailing extra. The per-month extra column stays.

This folder builds on [intent/complete-loan-picture/](../complete-loan-picture/spec.md), [intent/arm-loan/](../arm-loan/spec.md), and [intent/ui-followups/](../ui-followups/spec.md). Where this spec changes a sentence of one of those, D16 names it and this spec wins. Everything else in them stands. The page still calls `buildReport`. `amortize.js` does not change.

## 2. Goals and non-goals

**Goals**

- G1. Store more than one scenario, switch among them, add one, and remove one after confirmation, without dropping a loan that is already saved.
- G2. Export the whole set as one JSON file and import a file in place of the set, rejecting a file that does not validate.
- G3. Define the prevailing extra, the modal principal-and-interest payment, and the prevailing monthly total, and show the total in the heading and in the scenario label.
- G4. Show the prevailing extra in the picture table and in the schedule, beside principal and interest, and keep the per-month extra column.
- G5. Draw a PI / TI / PE bar beside the prevailing amount.

**Non-goals**

- A typed scenario name, a duplicate button, or drag-to-reorder. The label is computed.
- Any change to `amortize.js`, `compound_interest_monthly.js`, `origination_fees.js`, `amortize.d.ts`, the two skills, `node --test`, or `.github/workflows/`.
- A second amortization formula, a change to ARM rate math, or a change to what the summary's Monthly payment and the picture's Principal and interest row mean (both stay the initial `monthly_payment_cents`).
- Removing the per-month Extra payment column, or summing the prevailing extra on a year row.
- A new storage key, a new npm dependency, or a network request. The file is downloaded and read in the browser.
- Bumping `apps/web/package.json` or `PAGES_POINT_VERSION`. That stays the owner's release step.
- Teaching the skill or the CLI about scenarios or the prevailing total.

**Release phasing.** Every requirement below is this release. Nothing is tagged later.

## 3. Principles

- P1. One walk. The prevailing figures come from one extra `buildReport` call whose extra map is the applied map and whose `arm` argument is the same object the page already passes. The schedule, the chart, and the summary keep the call they have today, which uses the requested extras. Neither call is a new formula.
- P2. A saved loan is kept. A version-1 object becomes the first scenario. A bad import does not replace the set. Reading storage does not write it. The published prototype only reads version 1, so the old key keeps the active scenario in that shape and the other scenarios live in a second key a version-1 save cannot overwrite.
- P3. Applied extra is what the walk applied (`extra_cents`), not the dollars typed in the cell and not a request Apply has not written.
- P4. The heading's dollar is the prevailing total, rounded the way the heading already rounds. The picture's existing total stays the sheet total.
- P5. The list is page-drawn. A native `<select>` cannot hold a trash icon or a + row.

## 4. Users and scenarios

The fresh loan is the Conventional visit: purchase price $600,000, 5% down, 30 years, note rate 7.375%, start month October 2026. Its picture is the default picture.

- A browser that already has one loan under `compound-amortization-v1` opens on that loan. The list has one row. Nothing was discarded.
- The owner adds a scenario. The new row is the fresh Conventional loan, and it is the one on screen. The previous loan is still in the list.
- The owner applies $100 in every month of the fresh loan. The heading moves from `$4,853` to `$4,953`. The bar grows a gold segment. Removing the only remaining scenario is not offered.
- The owner exports, clears the site data, and imports the file. Both scenarios are back, and the one that was selected is selected again.
- A file that is not a saved set leaves the loans on screen untouched.
- On the 7/1 ARM at 5.875%, the heading shows `$5,954`, the summary's Monthly payment stays `$3,371.77`, and the schedule's Payment column still shows `$3,371.77` for the first 84 months and `$5,037.71` from payment 85.

## 5. Functional requirements

1. **The set.** The page holds an ordered list of scenarios and an active index. The scenario on screen is the active one. Every field a scenario has today stays on that scenario: the draft (`price` in dollars, `down`, `years`, `rate`, `start`), `extras`, `applied`, `prefill`, `openYears`, `picture`, and `arm` when it has been stored. Edits keep saving as they do now, into the active scenario. A term change still drops months past the term from that scenario's `extras` and `applied`. Switching scenarios loads the chosen one's stored fields into the form, the schedule, the picture, and the ARM disclosure. An invalid keystroke, an extra or index edit that has not been committed, and a picture or ARM field error belong to the scenario being left and are discarded; the loaded scenario is its last saved fields, which parse. The active index is stored with the set. Each list row's label and bar are computed from that row's stored scenario, not from the on-screen draft.

2. **Storage.** Two keys. The legacy key is unchanged: `compound-amortization-v1`, or `VITE_STORAGE_KEY` when that is set. Production and the prototype share `compound-amortization-v1`. A feature preview keeps its own legacy key and does not read or write the production key. The set key is that same string with the trailing `-v1` replaced by `-v2` (`compound-amortization-v2`, or `compound-amortization-feat-<id>-v2`). The page's new functions are `loadStored` and `saveStored`. `loadScenario` and `saveScenario` stay the single-scenario helpers: `loadScenario` still reads a version-1 object from the legacy key, and `saveScenario`'s version-1 shape is what `saveStored` writes there for the active scenario.

   The set key holds version 2:

   ```json
   {
     "version": 2,
     "active": 0,
     "scenarios": [
       {
         "price": "600000",
         "down": "5",
         "years": "30",
         "rate": "7.375",
         "start": "2026-10",
         "extras": [],
         "applied": [],
         "prefill": { "monthly": "", "yearly": "", "month": 1, "open": false },
         "openYears": null,
         "picture": { }
       }
     ]
   }
   ```

   Each scenario object is the version-1 object without its own `version` field. `arm` is present only when that scenario has stored ARM, under the same rule as today. `applied` is written on every save. A missing or malformed `applied` on a scenario equals that scenario's `extras`. A missing `openYears` is null. A malformed picture falls back to the picture defaults and does not reject the scenario. A malformed `arm` is fixed mode with the ARM defaults and does not reject the scenario. Those three fallbacks are the ones load uses today.

   `loadStored` reads both keys and does not write. A valid set key is the set. A valid version-1 object in the legacy key, when there is no valid set, is `{ active: 0, scenarios: [that loan] }`. When both are valid, the legacy object is parsed with the same one-scenario rules. It differs from the active scenario when any of draft, extras, applied, prefill, openYears, picture, arm, or `armStored` is not equal, with the maps compared by entries. A difference replaces the active scenario and the other scenarios stay. Missing `applied` on the legacy object counts as equal to its extras, which is how that object already loads, so a legacy save that omitted `applied` does not look like an edit when the active scenario's applied map matches its extras. That is how an edit made by the published prototype, or by a rolled-back production build, comes back without deleting the rest of the set. The published prototype only accepts `version === 1`. A version-2 value in the legacy key would load there as the fresh visit, and the prototype's next edit would replace every scenario. The legacy key therefore stays version 1 forever: `saveStored` writes the active scenario there in today's version-1 shape, and writes the whole set to the set key. A missing set key, a non-JSON set, a version other than 2, an empty or non-array `scenarios`, or any scenario whose loan, `extras`, `prefill`, or `openYears` would be rejected today, ignores the set key. If the legacy key then also fails, the result is the fresh one-scenario set, which is today's fallback for a corrupt key. An `active` that is missing, not an integer, or outside the array is read as 0 and the scenarios are kept. The legacy bytes stay on disk until the next save. A save writes every scenario to the set key, not only the active one.

3. **The list.** The first control in `<main>`, above the `h1`, is a row with the scenario button, an Export button, and an Import button. The scenario button's id is `scenario`. It shows the active scenario's label (requirement 8) and the color bar (requirement 9), with `aria-haspopup="dialog"` and `aria-expanded`. Activating it opens a page-drawn dialog labeled `Loan scenarios`, anchored under the button. The dialog lists one row per scenario, in stored order. The active row is `aria-current="true"`. Choosing a row makes it active, closes the dialog, and returns focus to `#scenario`. Escape, and a pointer down outside the dialog and the button, close it without switching. The button's click toggles it.

   The + control is the last row inside the dialog, not a scenario and not at the top. Its visible text is `+` and its accessible name is `New scenario`. Activating it appends a fresh Conventional scenario (`defaultScenario` at the current clock, empty extras, default picture, ARM off) and makes it active, then closes the dialog and focuses `#scenario`. It does not copy the scenario that was on screen.

   Each scenario row has a trash button at the right edge, accessible name `Remove scenario`. It is an inline SVG icon, not a text link. Its computed opacity is 0 until the row is hovered or the row contains focus, when the opacity is 1. When the pointer cannot hover (`hover: none`), the opacity is 1 without a hover. The button stays in the tab order while its opacity is 0. Activating it does not switch scenarios. It opens a second dialog, `role="dialog"` and modal, labeled `Remove this scenario?`, rendered outside the list dialog so a pointer down on it is not an outside click of the list. The list stays open behind it. The confirm text includes that row's label, with buttons `Remove` and `Cancel`. Escape and Cancel close only the confirm dialog, leave the set unchanged, leave the list open, and return focus to that row's trash button. A pointer down outside both dialogs and the `#scenario` button closes both without removing. Remove deletes that scenario and closes both dialogs, then focuses `#scenario`. If the deleted row was active, the row that shifts into its index becomes active, or the new last row when the deleted row was last. If it was not active, the active scenario stays, and its index moves down by one when it was after the deleted row.

   While the confirm dialog is open, a click on `#scenario` does not toggle the list and does not close the confirm. The confirm stays until Remove, Cancel, Escape, or a pointer down outside both dialogs and the button.

   When the set has one scenario, that row has no trash button, and a remove does nothing. The set always has at least one scenario.

4. **Export and import.** Export downloads the version-2 document the next set-key save would write, every scenario and the active index, as `compound-loan-scenarios.json` (`application/json`). The bytes are `JSON.stringify` of that document. Import is a button labeled `Import` that clicks a hidden `<input type="file" accept="application/json,.json">`. After each `change`, the input's value is set back to empty so the same filename can be chosen again. `parseStoredSet(text)` is the pure check: it returns the set or a rejection and does not touch storage. A version-1 object that would load is valid and becomes the whole set: one scenario, active 0. A valid version-2 document replaces the set and selects its active index (or 0 when that index is not usable). On success the page shows that scenario, as a switch does, and `saveStored` runs immediately, so a reload keeps the imported set. A file that fails validation does not change the set or either key, and the page shows `This file is not a saved set of loan scenarios.` beside the buttons. A later successful import removes that sentence. Import replaces; it does not merge.

5. **Prevailing extra.** "Applied extra" here is the walk's `extra_cents`, not the dollars stored in the `applied` map. The map is only the input to the walk: `buildReport` (through `loanReport`) is called with that map, the picture's financed principal, and the same `arm` argument the page uses for that product. The months are `1 .. payoff_month` of that report. Fixed mode passes `undefined` for `arm` even when ARM terms are stored. For each of those rows, the applied extra is the row's `extra_cents`. Group the rows by that integer. A positive amount (cents greater than 0) is the prevailing extra when its count `c` and the month count `n` satisfy `c * 5 >= n * 4`. Otherwise the prevailing extra is 0. Exactly 80 percent qualifies. A zero amount is not a candidate. Two different positive amounts cannot both qualify. The requested map is not read. A cell that differs from the applied map does not change this figure until Apply. An uncommitted keystroke does not either. The payoff month counts, and its `extra_cents` is whatever the walk applied there, which may be smaller than the request; that smaller amount is its own group.

6. **Principal and interest in the total.** On that same applied-map report, the principal-and-interest payment in effect is, for a fixed loan, `monthly_payment_cents` on every month through `payoff_month`. It is not `principal_cents + interest_cents`, which is smaller on a payoff month. For an ARM it is the row's `payment_cents` on those months. The modal payment is the amount with the greatest count. A tie uses the amount whose earliest month is earlier. Months after `payoff_month` are not in the count; their `payment_cents` of 0 must not become the mode.

7. **Taxes and insurance.** TI is `taxCents + insuranceCents + fhaMipCents + pmiCents` from `buildPicture` on the current valid picture. Each term is 0 when the picture computes 0, including a stored 0 percent, Conventional's $0.00 FHA MIP, and PMI at 20 percent down or more. The upfront MIP field is not a monthly amount; it stays inside the financed principal, as it does today. An invalid picture keystroke keeps the last valid picture, so it does not zero TI.

8. **The total, the heading, and the label.** The prevailing monthly total in cents is the modal payment plus TI plus the prevailing extra. The heading's dollar figure becomes that total rounded with `headingDollarsFromCents` and shown with `formatWholeDollars`, in the same place as today's complete payment. Its accessible name changes from `Complete monthly payment` to `Prevailing monthly payment`. The rest of the heading line is unchanged: thousands, down payment, note rate, the ARM structure label when the product is ARM, and the product select. On the fresh fixed visit the figure is still `$4,853`.

   The scenario label is the same whole-dollar amount, then ` - `, then the facts joined by ` | `: the purchase price in thousands and `K`, the down-payment text and `% down`, the term as `{n} year` or `{n} years`, and `fixed` or the ARM structure from `armLabel` (`7/1` when the interval is 12 months, `7/6` when it is 6, otherwise `{years}/{months}mo`). The thousands text is `dollarsToThousandsText` of that scenario's stored price string, the same function the heading uses to show a valid price. The down text is the stored down string. The note rate is not in the label. Checked 2026-10-10: the heading's editable controls are `#price`, `#down`, `#rate`, and `#product`; the term is `#years`, below the heading; the structure label is computed text, not an input. The intent's constraint list names price, down, term, and fixed or the ARM structure, and does not name the rate, so the rate stays on the heading only. The label uses the last saved loan, so an invalid keystroke in the heading does not rewrite it. The fresh fixed label is `$4,853 - 600K | 5% down | 30 years | fixed`. The 5.875% 7/1 label is `$5,954 - 600K | 5% down | 30 years | 7/1`. Inactive rows use this same function on their own stored fields.

9. **The color bar.** Immediately after the prevailing amount in the heading (before `/ month`) and immediately after the amount on `#scenario` and on each list row, a bar shows three shares of the cent total. Left to right they are PI, TI, and PE.

   | Segment | What it is | Color | Token |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | PI | The modal principal-and-interest payment | green `#1b7a4d` | `--prevailing-pi` |
   | TI | Taxes, home insurance, PMI, and MIP | navy `#2a4365` | `--prevailing-ti` |
   | PE | The prevailing extra | gold `#d9a441` | `--prevailing-pe` |

   The bar is 4.5rem wide and 0.5rem tall in the heading, on `#scenario`, and in each row. A segment whose cents are 0 is not rendered, so it takes no width and no border. The others fill the bar. Each rendered segment's `flex-grow` is its cent value and its `flex-basis` is 0, so the widths are the shares and they sum to the bar. A valid loan has a positive modal payment (`loanCents` must be greater than 0), so the PI segment is always present. The heading bar is `role="img"` and its accessible name lists all three amounts through `formatMoney`, including a $0.00 part that has no segment: `Principal and interest $3,936.85, taxes and insurance $916.25, prevailing extra $0.00`. The bars inside `#scenario` and the rows are `aria-hidden="true"`, so they do not merge into those buttons' names. `#scenario`'s accessible name is the label text only. The bar has no text nodes, so the heading's collected text does not gain words. The fresh fixed bar is green and navy only. After F2 it has a gold segment as well.

10. **The picture table.** After the existing `Total monthly payment` row, three rows are added, in order, each a money row:

    | `data-line` | Label | Amount |
    |---|---|---|
    | `prevailing-principal-and-interest` | Prevailing principal and interest | the modal payment |
    | `prevailing-extra` | Prevailing extra | the prevailing extra |
    | `prevailing-monthly-total` | Prevailing monthly total | the cent total |

    `Principal and interest` stays `monthly_payment_cents` of the no-extra, no-ARM picture call. `Total monthly payment` stays that initial payment plus TI. On the fresh fixed visit those two existing rows are unchanged (`$3,936.85` and `$4,853.10`), and the three new rows read `$3,936.85`, `$0.00`, and `$4,853.10`.

11. **The schedule.** Both column layouts gain a `Prevailing extra` column immediately after Interest and before Index (ARM) or before Extra payment (fixed). Index stays immediately before Extra payment. Each month cell shows the scenario's prevailing extra through `formatMoney`, the same amount on every month. The year cell is empty. It is not a sum. The Extra payment column, its input, and `data-edited` are unchanged. Fixed mode goes from eight columns to nine. ARM mode goes from eleven to twelve. The table still ends at `payoff_month` of the requested-extra report, which is the schedule on screen. The prevailing column's amount comes from the applied-map report, so a blue cell does not change it.

12. **What does not change.** The summary's Monthly payment, its ARM note, Highest rate, Highest payment, Extra principal paid, Interest saved, and Months saved. The chart. Saved by extra on a month or a year. The start-month picker. Apply still replaces the extra column. The ARM terms, the Index cell, and the ARM rate math. `node --test`. The workflows. The legacy key and the feature-preview key scheme. The picture's Principal and interest row, which stays `monthly_payment_cents`.

## 7. Design decisions

**D1 — The legacy key stays version 1, and the set lives in its `-v2` twin.** Checked 2026-10-10 in `loan.ts`: `STORAGE_KEY` is `import.meta.env.VITE_STORAGE_KEY ?? "compound-amortization-v1"`, and `loadScenario` returns the fresh visit unless `version === 1`. Production and the prototype both use the default, and the prototype is not republished, so it will keep that check. Writing version 2 into the shared key would make the prototype, and a rolled-back production, load the fresh visit and overwrite every scenario on the next edit. That fails "do not wipe a saved loan." The legacy key therefore stays the active scenario in today's version-1 shape, which is what those readers already understand. The full set is the same key with `-v1` replaced by `-v2`, which a version-1 reader never opens. A preview's `VITE_STORAGE_KEY` (`compound-amortization-feat-<pull-request-number>-v1`, or the branch sha form) gets the same twin, so previews still do not share production storage. A version-1 object with no set key is scenario 0. When both keys exist and the version-1 loan differs, it replaces only the active scenario, which is an edit made by the prototype or an old build. Reading does not write. Putting the set only in a brand-new unrelated key, and ignoring the legacy key, was rejected because the loan already saved would not load.

**D2 — Export is the whole set. Import replaces, and a bad file does not.** One file is what the owner asked to move between browsers. Merging an import with the loans already on the page was rejected because a file is a saved set, not a patch. Load's failure mode is the fresh visit, which would discard the current loans if import used it as the replacement. Import therefore uses the same acceptance rules and, on failure, keeps the set and says so. A version-1 file is accepted because load accepts it, and it becomes the only scenario.

**D3 — Prevailing extra is applied `extra_cents` on at least 80 percent of the months through payoff.** The page schedule ends at `payoff_month`. Months after payoff are not rows on screen, and their applied extra is 0, so they are not in the denominator. Counting them would call a constant extra that paid the loan off early "not prevailing." The 80 percent test is `c * 5 >= n * 4`, which accepts 288 of 360 and rejects 287 of 360 (F4) without a float. The amount is `extra_cents` from a walk of the applied map. The requested map still drives the schedule, so a blue cell is not yet this extra. Checked 2026-10-10: $100 in every month of the 7.375% loan is 10000 cents on all 331 months through payoff (F2); $100 in months 1–12 is 12 of 358 and does not qualify (F3); the 7% example is 10000 cents on all 332 months (F7).

**D4 — The payment in effect is the scheduled payment, and a tie uses the earlier month.** On a fixed loan every month through payoff is `monthly_payment_cents`. Adding that month's principal and interest would drop the payoff month into a different group. On an ARM the row's `payment_cents` is that scheduled payment; it is 0 only after payoff, and those rows are outside the slice. Checked 2026-10-10: the 7/1 worst case is 84 months at 337177 and 276 at 503771, so the mode is 503771 (F5). A 15-year fixed period ties 180 against 180, and month 1's 337177 wins over month 181's 454645 (F6). Both counts use the applied-map report so "schedule months" means one slice. While a cell is blue, the Payment column can differ from this mode; the mode describes the applied plan.

**D5 — TI is the four picture cents, and an empty keystroke is not a zero.** `buildPicture` already computes tax, home insurance, FHA MIP, and conventional PMI. Conventional monthly MIP is 0. PMI is 0 at 20 percent down. The upfront MIP percent is not a monthly charge. The picture treats an empty field as invalid and keeps the last valid draft, so the prevailing total does the same. A stored `0` is 0 cents.

**D6 — The heading shows the prevailing total in whole dollars. The sheet lines stay.** `headingDollarsFromCents` is the heading's rounding. On F1 the total is 485310 cents, which is today's `$4,853`, so the fresh visit does not move. On F5 the mode is the post-reset payment, so the ARM heading moves from `$4,288` to `$5,954`. The summary and the picture's Principal and interest stay the initial payment, which is what `intent/arm-loan/` says `monthly_payment_cents` is. The picture gains three rows so the new total is visible next to the sheet total.

**D7 — The schedule shows the prevailing extra beside principal and interest, and keeps Extra payment.** The column is the same amount on every month row because it is a property of the scenario, not a cash flow. The year cell is empty so it is not read as a sum of months. Putting it after Interest leaves Index immediately before Extra payment, which is where `intent/arm-loan/` placed the Index cell.

**D8 — The label is the total, a hyphen, and four facts. The rate stays on the heading.** Checked 2026-10-10 against `app.tsx`: the heading line is `{thousands}K | {down}% down | {rate}% {structure when ARM} {fixed|ARM} = ${whole dollars} / month`. The term is the Term field under that line, not in it. The intent's constraint list names price, down, term, and fixed or the ARM structure, and does not name the rate, so the rate stays on the heading and out of the label. The thousands text is `dollarsToThousandsText` of the stored price. The structure text is `armLabel`: `7/1`, `7/6`, or `7/1mo`. The word ARM is the product select and is not repeated in the label. The separator is ` - `. The facts use ` | `, matching the heading. The label is built from the saved scenario so a half-typed price does not name it. Inactive rows use their own stored fields.

**D9 — One more `buildReport` call, not a new formula.** The call uses the applied map, the financed principal, and the `arm` object `loanReport` already builds. Fixed mode passes no `arm`. The CLI and the library are untouched.

**D10 — The list is page-drawn, and + is the last row.** A native select cannot show a trash icon or a + control. The + sits at the bottom so it is not above the first loan and does not take the place of a scenario. The top of the dialog was the other place the request allowed; the bottom is the one that reads as "add after the list." The new scenario is `defaultScenario`, not a copy of the current loan. A copy would silently duplicate every extra. "Starts a new scenario" is the fresh Conventional visit.

**D11 — Trash is on the right, shown on hover, and the last scenario stays.** The icon is at the row's right edge. Opacity 0 until `:hover` or `:focus-within` keeps it out of the way and still available from the keyboard. The check is computed opacity, not Playwright's `toBeVisible`, which treats opacity 0 as visible. `(hover: none)` shows it always, because a phone has no hover and could not otherwise remove a loan. Remove asks first, in a second page-drawn dialog, not `window.confirm`. The confirm dialog sits outside the list so its own pointer events are not an outside click, the list stays open behind it, and Escape closes only the confirm. A click on `#scenario` while the confirm is open does nothing to either dialog. Remove then closes both and focuses `#scenario`. The last row does not render the button, and the remove function refuses a set of one.

**D12 — The bar is green, navy, and gold, and a zero share is absent.** The three colors are green `#1b7a4d` (PI), navy `#2a4365` (TI), and gold `#d9a441` (PE), the hex values already used for `--principal`, `--balance`, and `--remaining`. They are new tokens so a later chart change does not recolor the bar. The green segment is the whole principal-and-interest share, not the chart's principal band. The bar is 4.5rem by 0.5rem so `flex-basis: 0` still has a container. `flex-grow` set to the cent value fills it without a percentage rounding gap. A 0-cent segment is omitted, not drawn at zero width. A valid loan's modal payment is positive, so the bar is never empty. The bar sits beside the amount in the heading and in the list, which are the two places that amount is shown. Only the heading bar is named, so a bar inside a button does not merge into that button's accessible name. The heading name carries the three amounts so the colors are not the only encoding. No text nodes, so `headingText` in the Playwright specs stays a walk of inputs, the select, and text.

**D13 — A bad active index is not a bad file. One bad scenario is.** An out-of-range `active` would otherwise discard a set the owner can still use, so it is read as 0. A scenario that fails the loan parse means the file is not a set load would accept, so the whole file is rejected. `parseStoredSet` is that predicate for import. `loadStored` uses the same scenario checks. A corrupt set key is ignored and the legacy key is tried; if that also fails, storage load falls back to the fresh visit, which is today's behavior for a corrupt key, and the next edit would then save that fresh visit. That is the corrupt-key rule, not a partial rescue of a broken file. Import does not use that fallback as a replacement: a bad file keeps both keys. P2's "a saved loan is kept" means a file or a legacy object that parses. It does not mean a half-parsed envelope is merged.

**D14 — The set in memory is the whole set.** The page keeps every scenario while it edits the active one. A save writes them all. Holding only the active scenario and saving it back would delete the others on the first edit after a version-2 load.

**D15 — This release is the whole feature.** The list, the + control, the trash icon, export, import, the prevailing total, the heading, the picture rows, the schedule column, and the bar all ship now. None is tagged later.

**D16 — What this supersedes.** Only these points, and only for the page. `intent/complete-loan-picture/` requirement 2, requirement 12, and D21: the heading dollar is the prevailing monthly total, and its accessible name is `Prevailing monthly payment`, not `Complete monthly payment`. `picture.spec.ts` follows that name. On the fresh fixed visit the dollars are still `$4,853`. The picture's Principal and interest row and its Total monthly payment row stay that spec's figures. `intent/arm-loan/` section 4 and AC9's heading after the rate is set to 5.875, and AC11's production check of that heading: `$4,288` becomes `$5,954` (F5). The structure label, the summary's `$3,371.77`, and the highest payment `$5,037.71` stay. The fixed heading at that rate stays `$4,288`, which is F5's initial payment 337177 plus TI 91625, not F6. F6 is the tie, and it happens to total the same cents. `intent/arm-loan/` AC9's "eight columns," and the eleven-column cell positions, become nine and twelve (requirement 11). `arm.spec.ts` assertions of `thead th` counts 8 and 11, and an Index cell at `nth(6)`, follow the new counts. `intent/complete-loan-picture/` requirement 6, `intent/arm-loan/` requirement 16, and `intent/ui-followups/` requirement 8: the legacy key and the per-scenario fields stay, including `applied` and `arm`, still version 1 on that key; the set around them is the version-2 twin key. `intent/ui-followups/` requirement 11's "the heading" is unchanged except for the dollar and its name, as D6 says. Its AC5 cell indexes move right by one because of the new column: the fixed year row's Saved by extra cell becomes the seventh cell, and the ARM year row's becomes the tenth. `AGENTS.md` sentences that say eight fixed columns and eleven ARM columns are replaced, not left beside the new counts.
